Data source & methodology

Word vectors sourced from GloVe: Global Vectors for Word Representation (Pennington et al., Stanford NLP, 2014) — specifically the GloVe 6B 50-dimensional model trained on Wikipedia 2014 and Gigaword 5 (6B tokens, 400K vocab).

Each word is represented as a 50-dimensional vector of floating point numbers. To visualize these vectors in three dimensions, Principal Component Analysis (PCA) was applied to the 17-word matrix across 5 semantic categories (animals, cities, emotions, technology, food). PCA finds the directions of maximum variance in the high-dimensional space and projects the data onto the top 3 principal components (PC1, PC2, PC3). These three components capture 96.9% of the total variance in the original 50-dimensional space. Coordinates are the raw unscaled PCA output — no normalization or rescaling was applied — so relative distances and magnitudes between words are fully preserved. The data is centered at the PCA centroid (not the origin). The axes (PC1, PC2, PC3) are linear combinations of the original 50 dimensions and do not have simple human-interpretable labels — which is precisely the point.
